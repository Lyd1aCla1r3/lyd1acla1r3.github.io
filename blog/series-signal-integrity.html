Part 1: Wave and Loss Foundations

The electromagnetic wave is the carrier of all digital information. Part 1 traces the physics of wave propagation on PCB transmission lines, the frequency content of digital signals, characteristic impedance and reflection coefficients, magnetic coupling and crosstalk, and the frequency-dependent conductor and dielectric losses that attenuate high-speed channels. (5 chapters)

Volume 2: Measurement, SerDes, and Coherent Optics (Concise First Edition)

Parts 2 through 6 advance from board-level physics into laboratory characterization and multi-gigabit transceiver design in a concise reference format across Chapters 6 to 21. Download Volume 2 PDF (Ch 6-21) →

Part 2: Discontinuities and Signal Integrity Measurement

Physical discontinuities reshape traveling waves, and measurement instruments capture the resulting channel behavior. Part 2 covers return-path dynamics and via parasitics, time-domain reflectometry, S-parameter matrix formalism with vector network analysis, and the Smith chart, which maps reflection data onto the impedance plane. (4 chapters)

Part 3: Jitter Analysis

Edge timing uncertainty is the ultimate limiter of serial link performance. Part 3 decomposes total jitter into random and deterministic components, derives the Dual-Dirac model for BER extrapolation to unmeasurable error ratios, and details the test pattern selection and spectral techniques that isolate each jitter source. (3 chapters)

Part 4: Equalization and RX Architecture

The channel degrades signal quality, and equalization reconstructs it. Part 4 derives transmitter feed-forward equalization, continuous-time linear equalization, and decision feedback equalization as three complementary stages of a modern link budget, then extends the framework to PAM4 multi-level signaling and Gray coding. (3 chapters)

Part 5: SerDes Architecture and Clocking

The serializer/deserializer is the complete system that converts parallel logic into serial analog transmission and back. Part 5 covers SerDes architecture and eye diagram measurement, CDR and PLL loop dynamics including phase noise analysis, and the line coding, forward error correction, and protocol framing layers that sit between the physical channel and usable data throughput. (3 chapters)

Part 6: Coherent Optics

Coherent optical transmission extends high-speed link physics into the optical domain. Part 6 examines how electro-optic modulators manipulate the refractive index of lithium niobate to encode digital data into the phase and amplitude of orthogonal electromagnetic fields, and how wideband signal analyzers isolate the resulting physical-layer distortions. (3 chapters)
