Section 02 / Customer

A History of SE Engagement

Tales from the Bench

01 / HDMI ARC/eARC

Standard
HDMI ARC and eARC compliance
Problem
Immature software, hardware and software incompatibilities, and incomplete documentation blocked characterization of a new device on the ARC and eARC test stations.
Action
Brought up the stations ahead of IP availability, then supplied debug data to a joint effort with Keysight and BitifEye.
Impact
The collaboration produced clearer vendor configuration documents and a formal support path for third-party equipment integration.

02 / USB Type-C/BC1.2

Standard
USB Type-C and BC1.2 compliance
Problem
BC1.2 test sequences reported failures on a wearable that did not match observed device behavior.
Action
Cross-checked results on alternate equipment and worked with vendor FAEs. Consulted the USB-IF Compliance Administrator and the BC1.2 Working Group Chair on the specification and its Engineering Change Notice.
Impact
Confirmed that the failures originated in the test sequences, cleared the product, and aligned the compliance scope with its market positioning.

03 / Instrument Correlation

Standard
VNA and TDR measurement correlation
Problem
The lab required broader measurement capability within a fixed budget, along with consistent data across instrument vendors.
Action
Iterated instrument and accessory configurations with a Keysight FAE, then coordinated onsite bring-up and verification.
Impact
Correlated TDR and VNA results wherever their measurements overlapped, which allowed data to move cleanly between instruments.

04 / Remote Access

Standard
Remote access and lab software management
Problem
Test continuity depended on onsite presence, and software versions and licenses were not tracked in one place.
Action
Consulted vendor FAEs on remote access options and coordinated with IT teams on security requirements. Built a centralized inventory of station software, versions, and licenses.
Impact
Secure remote access to instruments was approved, FAEs gained the ability to troubleshoot remotely, and license gaps informed budget planning.

Interactions with validation engineers are grounded in mutual laboratory experience rather than traditional vendor dynamics. This peer-to-peer approach fosters immediate technical trust.
