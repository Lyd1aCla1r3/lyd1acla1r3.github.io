Section 03 / Architecture

How I See the Role

My perspective on this role starts with tracing the full chain: identifying the computational pattern generating a demand, the physical bottleneck it creates, and the measurement approach needed to validate the standard designed to address it. Advances in AI have placed an incredible strain on the physical infrastructure, so I am using the criteria for neural network training as an example of how I approach customer engagement.

Workloads

Models have outgrown individual processors, and the parallelization strategies predict where infrastructure pressure will concentrate. By recognizing these patterns early, an SE can guide customers toward the right measurements before performance problems surface in production.

Operational Impact

Each workload pattern above maps to a specific physical constraint. Tracing a performance concern backward to its computational root cause, and then forward to the right measurement strategy, is what separates a transactional support visit from a strategic technical engagement. This kind of causal reasoning also positions the SE to educate customers on what to watch for next, as workloads evolve and infrastructure ages.

Physical Layer Standards

Every standard listed below exists because a specific operational bottleneck outgrew the physical layer technology. An SE who can track this evolution becomes more than a vendor resource. That SE becomes part of how the customer decides where to invest, what methodology to adopt, and how to prepare their infrastructure for the workloads coming next.
