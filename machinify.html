Tool-agnostic logic over vendor lock-in.

Each pipeline I've built is architected around tool-agnostic logic. I abstract the underlying reasoning of a workflow so that implementation details can change without disrupting the architecture. The field moves weekly, and the systems I build survive that churn because the decision logic is decoupled from any single vendor or framework. When a new capability earns adoption, I swap the integration layer while the governance, the guardrails, the structured outputs, and the audit trail remain intact.

That instinct was shaped by fifteen years of evaluating test equipment vendors, where selecting instrumentation suites and compliance modules meant reading specifications in detail, researching competing designs and their trade-offs, and making clear go/no-go decisions based on reliability, scalability, and long-term standards alignment. I apply that same evaluation discipline now when choosing between agent SDKs, reasoning models, and retrieval strategies, and I hold strong, data-backed opinions on when each one earns its cost.

Every decision must be traceable and defensible.

My AI doc writer agent translates raw engineering signals into publication-ready documentation through a seven-stage architecture with isolated personas handling generation, evaluation, routing, and fact-checking under strict temperature boundaries. What enters the prompt at every stage is what I obsess over most, because context engineering determines whether a system produces a defensible finding or an eloquent hallucination.

Context overflow was one of the earliest production failures I had to solve. Rather than flooding downstream mechanisms with full payloads, I engineered slicing and deflation strategies that extract only the relevant signals in stages, preserving full context fidelity without exceeding token budgets. That same discipline carries through to the grounding audit: a zero-temperature fact-checking pass that traces every claim to the raw source material and mandates gap annotations for missing context rather than allowing the model to fabricate. A recovery engine catches failed quality checks, and a circuit breaker trips after two retries to halt the process rather than silently degrade output. Nothing ships that I cannot reproduce and defend.

Building the evaluation harness first.

These pipelines are living systems, not static deployments. Output data and team feedback are ingested automatically to inform revisions. I run structured A/B testing and evidence-backed parameter sweeps across prompt configurations, scoring thresholds, and matching algorithms to drive each iteration toward consistent, defensible output. My analytics deflection engine went through a 200-combination parameter sweep to eliminate subjective categorization from its triage taxonomy.

I develop exhaustively for edge cases because that is where production systems actually break. I build every evaluation harness before I build the agent it governs, because an agent that passes its own cherry-picked tests but fails on adversarial input is worse than no agent at all. Each pipeline run produces an audit file with specific reason codes that feed directly into the next development cycle. During development itself, I perform tree-of-thought and adversarial self-audits to ground and constrain pipeline behavior, stress-testing assumptions before they harden into architecture.

Zero-risk philosophy and mathematical domain depth.

Fifteen years of hardware compliance testing at certification level taught me that "mostly right" is indistinguishable from wrong when the result must survive scrutiny. That conviction governs my approach to privacy and safety, which are non-negotiable structural commitments. My analytics deflection engine scrubs all personally identifiable information through a strict k-anonymity model before extracting any insight, because in domains with regulatory exposure, data that should never have been visible cannot be retroactively unseen. My link governance engine enforces a zero-risk philosophy where only a verified subset of failure categories qualifies for automated remediation. I do not accept arbitrary defaults; every threshold and scoring weight is calibrated through evidence-backed sweeps and validated against industry standards.

The domain knowledge runs deep as well. I have authored a 24-chapter, 100-page ebook building an autoregressive Decoder-only Transformer from scratch, calculating the entire forward pass by hand, along with a completed companion series on Byte Pair Encoding tokenization and an ongoing series on vector embeddings. Surface-level familiarity with how these models work produces surface-level engineering, and I go deeper because the architecture decisions that matter most happen at the layer where intuition meets mathematics.
